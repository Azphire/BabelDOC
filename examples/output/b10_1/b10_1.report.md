First batch of the five day repair cycle (b10.1–b10.5 + F3). Five items were
planned; four landed as fixes and the fifth is a determination that says the
plan's two candidate mechanisms are both wrong and that the fix it named cannot
be built where it said to build it.

Replay: four samples, whole documents, out of the project cache. Every request
this run built is byte for byte the request the F2 run built — 134, 161, 166 and
107 of them — which is the batch's conservation claim in its strongest form:
none of these five changes reaches the translation path. The evidence written

## T1 — the flattened drop cap no longer decides where its paragraph starts

`drop_cap.flatten` rebuilt the merged run's box, and the paragraph's, as the
union of every character in it. An enlarged initial stands proud of the line it
sits beside, so that union's start edge was the initial's and the stage set the
re-typeset paragraph from there — a whole initial's height above the column
beside it.

The merged box now takes its start edge from the union of the *tail* characters,
which is the text the initial opens; the horizontal extent still covers both.
Which vertical edge starts a paragraph is read off the characters (the first of
them against the last) rather than declared, so no coordinate direction is
written into the module — assertion 02c drives it both ways over a synthetic
pair.

Measured in ink, off the committed page images, against the first paragraph of
the column beside it:

| case | F2 gap | b10.1 gap | font size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Courier-en p4 | 31.68 pt | 0.00 pt | 9.20 pt |
| Courier-en p5 | 16.32 pt | 0.00 pt | 9.20 pt |
| Courier-en p7 | 16.32 pt | 0.00 pt | 9.20 pt |
| FD-en-v2 p8 | — | — | — |

FD page 8 was named in the plan and needed no change: its box is identical
before and after the merge, because the initial there does not stand above the
first line, and its page carries no second column of running text to be level
with (the left half is a photograph). The gate asserts that case as "the pass
left it exactly as it found it".

Both boxes are now in the sidecar as `box_before` / `box_after`, and
`configs/drop_cap.json` declares the two fields.

## T2 — the single line heading policy is the target language's

`title_min_scale` and `on_floor` were set for translating into Chinese, where a
headline can be squeezed to two thirds and still read. Courier-zh translates the
other way, and the same policy squeezed eleven of its headings to between 0.556
and 1.0 — five of them below 0.8, small enough to see — and raised one at 0.28
for a human.

`configs/title_typeset.json` now carries `title_min_scale_by_target` and
`on_floor_by_target`, keyed by language prefix, resolved longest prefix first
with the flat key as the fallback, exactly as the sentence profiles resolve a
language. English declares `{"en": 0.8}` and `{"en": "wrap"}`; Chinese declares
neither and reads the flat keys unchanged. Every value of either table is
checked against the vocabulary or the range its flat sibling is checked against,
and `spec_check_b9_2` refuses a table that breaks either.

The `wrap` landing was completed: a heading that reaches the floor under it
keeps the wrapped rendering the stage produced, records `disposition: wrap`, and
does not enter the escalation table. A heading with duplicate layers still
consumes the deduplication first and then wraps.

| --- | --- | --- |
| policy | `escalate`, floor 0.55 | `wrap`, floor 0.80 |
| set on one line | 11 (five below 0.8) | 2 (both at or above 0.8) |

## T3 — AramcoWorld p5#17: the determination, and why nothing was built

The plan required the mechanism to be determined before anything was changed,
and named two candidates plus "anything else — stop and report". The answer is
the third.

**The question the plan asked.** Does the single line branch write `kept` back to
the paragraph before rendering? **It does.** `_fit_single_line`
([title_typeset.py:594](../../../babeldoc/magazine/title_typeset.py)) calls
`_set_runs(paragraph, runs)` at the head of every attempt, before `_render`, and
`_process_title` hands it `kept`. Candidate (a) — measured deduplicated,
rendered original — is excluded, in the syntax tree (assertion 03a).

**Candidate (b) is excluded too.** It required the heading to have reached the
floor and been restored after a failed render, which would have emptied its
duplicate findings. The record says `disposition: single_line`, `scale: 1.0`,
two duplicate findings, no `restored` (assertion 03b).

**What is actually there.** The deduplication landed. Page 5 draws the display
line exactly once: two spans, `希贾兹铁路` and `?`, at 47.48 pt, from x 30.1 to
x 290.0. There is no second layer of text anywhere on that page.

What clips it is **occlusion, not overprint**. The page carries an image block at
[199.9, 41.3, 561.2, 121.2] drawn after the heading, covering everything right
of x ≈ 200 — which is why the reader sees `希贾兹铁` and half a glyph. In the
source, that image is the gradient the headline's *second* paint was drawn
through: the input draws `RAILWAY?` full width at x 47.8–570.4 and then `ILWAY?`
again at x 201.4–570.4 masked by that image. The title pass drops the second
text paint. The image is not a paragraph, so nothing drops it, and the page
rebuild emits it on its own, over the one surviving heading.

So the defect is a cross-object one — a duplicate *text* layer dropped without
the *graphic* that belonged to the same paint — and it cannot be closed inside
`title_typeset.py`, which is where T3 said the change had to be. Under the
plan's clause (c) nothing was built. Assertion 03c freezes all three facts, so
the finding fails the moment either half of it moves.

The F2 report read this as "the two overprinted and clipped"; that reading is
wrong and this supersedes it.

## T4 — same form parentheticals folded, deterministically

Two rounds of prompt work did not stop the engine glossing a name with itself,
so it is closed mechanically. New module `babeldoc/magazine/paren_dedup.py`,
config `configs/paren_dedup.json`, switch `magazine_paren_dedup` (up by
default), run between the translation being written back and the typesetting
stage — the one window where the text is final and the geometry is not.

The rule: a parenthetical whose content, after NFKC and outer whitespace, is
exactly how the text immediately before it ends, is removed along with any
whitespace run standing between them. Case is deliberately not folded, so an
initialism beside the words it abbreviates survives. `max_span_chars` (40,
ranged) stops the fold on a long parenthetical, which is more likely a real
apposition. Both halves of a paragraph's text — its compositions, which is what
the stage sets, and its `unicode` — are folded by the same rule read over each,
and the record reports each count separately, because the two are not always the
same string at that point in the pipeline (Courier-en `p7#3` is one such case:
folded in the compositions, and so in the output, while its `unicode` carries
style markup that the rule does not match).

| sample | paragraphs folded | spans | same form left on target pages (F2 → b10.1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Courier-en | 1 | `阿甘树` | 1 → 0 |
| FD-en-v2 | 1 | `Josh Lipsky` | 1 → 0 |
| AramcoWorld-en-v2 | 6 | `Khakimov`, `Edhem Eldem`, `Paul Binski` ×2, `Benjamin Claude Brower`, `David S. Powers`, `Eric Tagliacozzo` | 7 → 0 |
| Courier-zh | 3 | `Marcelo Silva de Sousa` ×2, `David Jefferson` | 2 → 0 |

Negative side: `哈基莫夫（Khakimov）` on AramcoWorld page 4 is still there in the
produced PDF, and a constructed gloss of the same shape is refused by the rule.
`Binski（Paul Binski）` is also still there, and correctly so — the content is
not what the text before it ends with, so it is not a same form parenthetical
and this rule is not the one that answers it.

## The sweep, and what this batch's green means

The full `run_all` over the whole history was measured here and does not fit a
per-batch discipline: 147 minutes of gate time across 27 of 34 gates before the
run was stopped by hand, with the 28th still going. The log and its profile are
in `profiling/`.

Under W-B10-04 the gates are split for the rest of the cycle. Every gate now
declares a module level `GATE_SET`, and `run_all --set {all,fast,sweep}` selects
on it. The declaration is mechanical rather than a stopwatch reading: `sweep`
where the gate asks the artifact builder for a document, because a cold slot
re-runs the pipeline over a sample to answer one assertion, and `fast` where it
answers from stubs it builds itself and from evidence a batch froze. A gate that
declares nothing is refused at the start rather than defaulted into either set.
`--set` is a different axis from the existing `--fast`, which narrows the
assertions inside a gate rather than the list of gates; both compose.

The split lands 18 gates in `sweep` (140.9 measured minutes) and 16 in `fast`.
The fast set was then run end to end: **16/16 green in 12.7 minutes**
(`profiling/run_all.b10_1.set_fast.log`). So **this batch's gate condition is
`spec_check_b10_1` plus the fast set**, and the sweep set runs once, on F3. The
cost is stated in the waiver: a regression an assertion in the sweep set would
catch is invisible between batches and surfaces at F3 at the latest.

## Conservation

- Page counts and per page paragraph counts on all fourteen target pages equal
  F2's.
- Every request equals F2's, request for request, in all three request groups.
- Paragraph text differs from F2 only at the eleven folded paragraphs and the
  two AramcoWorld page 4 paragraphs whose answers came back resampled.
- Both comparisons are frozen beside the run -- `conservation.json` for the
  shape, `parity.json` for the requests -- because the working directories they
  were made from are not tracked and the retention policy eventually takes them.
  The gate asserts off the frozen records and checks them against the workspace
  for as long as the workspace can still answer.
- No sample sent more API requests than its F2 run did.

## What is left

- **AramcoWorld p5 headline.** Open, with the mechanism now known: a duplicate
  text layer is dropped while the graphic that belonged to the same paint is
  not. Answering it means reaching a page's graphics from the heading pass, or
  reaching the heading's layers from whatever owns the graphic — neither is a
  change to `title_typeset.py` and neither is in this cycle's scope as declared.
- **`unicode` and compositions can disagree.** A paragraph whose `unicode`
  carries style markup is folded in its compositions and not in its `unicode`.
  The output is right either way; a detector reading `unicode` would see the
  stale text. Recorded per paragraph in the sidecar rather than papered over.
- **A few uncached requests, and they are the repair loop's.** Every sample
  still sends between one and three requests the cache does not answer, in this
  run as in F2, which sent between one and six. They are not the translator's:
  `parity.json` reads `translate_tracking.json`, whose only groups are `page`,
  `cross_page` and `cross_column` -- there is no `repair` group in it -- and
  every request of all three is byte identical to F2's. What the cache does not
  answer is the ReAct loop: one decision request per sample carries
  `from_cache: false` in `react_repair.report.json`, and the LLM backed repair
  actions beside it (`translate_orphan_lines`, twice on FD) account for the
  rest. The parity record now names both the groups it read and the groups the
  tracking held, and the gate fails if a group appears that the comparison did
  not read, so a `repair` group arriving later cannot be left out in silence.
  Why those decision requests miss a warm cache was not investigated here.
